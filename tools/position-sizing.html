Position Size Calculator

Size a position from your account balance, chosen loss budget and stop price.

Enter values to calculate.

How it works

Risk budget = balance × risk % / 100. Quantity = risk budget ÷ |entry − stop|.

Worked example

With a 1,000 USDT balance, 1% risk and entry at 100 with a stop at 95, the loss budget is 10 USDT. The calculated quantity is 2 units, worth 200 USDT at entry.

Assumptions

This is a price-distance estimate for linear USDT contracts. It excludes fees, slippage, funding and gaps. A stop order does not guarantee its execution price. Margin requirements are separate from the loss budget.

How to use the crypto position size calculator

  1. Enter your account balance in USDT, not the desired position value.
  2. Choose the percentage of that balance you are modeling as price risk.
  3. Enter an intended entry and a different stop price.
  4. Read quantity as coin units. Check exchange quantity increments before using that number elsewhere.

Compare a wider and tighter stop

Hypothetical example: with balance 1,000 and risk 1%, the budget stays 10 USDT. Entry 100 and stop 95 give quantity 2 and entry notional 200. Moving the stop to 98 produces quantity 5 and notional 500. A tighter stop increases the calculated exposure for the same planned price loss; it does not make execution safer.

Can I calculate a short position?

Yes. The formula uses the absolute entry-to-stop distance. Entry 100 and stop 105 also give a distance of 5. The calculator does not infer whether your proposed stop is appropriate for your trade direction.

Why is margin different from risk?

Margin supports exposure; the risk budget here models a move to your chosen stop. Neither value guarantees a maximum realized loss. The calculator does not check liquidation, available collateral or whether an order can be filled.
