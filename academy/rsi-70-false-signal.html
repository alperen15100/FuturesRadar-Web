RSI 70 False Signals: When Overbought Does Not Reverse

A move above RSI 70 can be a poor standalone reversal signal because strong directional markets can keep recent gains dominant for an extended period. The threshold does not estimate the probability, timing or size of a reversal.

What makes the signal “false”?

If someone interprets the first cross above 70 as an automatic short trigger and price continues higher, the trading rule failed—not the RSI calculation. RSI correctly described strong recent momentum; the unsupported assumption was that overbought must immediately reverse.

Three different situations

SituationWhat RSI 70 tells youWhat it does not tell you
Breakout trendRecent gains are strongWhere the trend ends
Range resistanceMomentum reached an elevated stateThat resistance must hold
Momentum weakeningCurrent RSI can still be highThat divergence guarantees reversal

Reduce interpretation errors

Separate the oscillator reading from the trade decision. Check whether price confirms or rejects a level, whether the broader trend is aligned, whether volume is expanding or fading, and where the idea becomes invalid. None of these removes uncertainty.
