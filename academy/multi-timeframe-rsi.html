Multi-Timeframe RSI Analysis

FuturesRadar Academy • Practical market guide • Not financial advice

Learn why comparing RSI across multiple timeframes can improve market context.

This guide is educational only. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell, long or short any asset. Crypto futures involve high risk.

Different windows answer different questions

A 15-minute RSI reflects a shorter sequence of price changes than an hourly RSI using the same period. Readings can disagree without either being incorrect. The higher interval can provide context while a lower interval describes short-term momentum.

A mixed reading

Suppose hourly RSI is 62 and 15-minute RSI is 38. That may describe a short-term pullback within a stronger recent hourly move. It does not guarantee a recovery. Inspect price levels and wait for a defined confirmation if your method requires one.

Match calculations first

This scanner uses a 14-change rolling calculation on 15-minute closes. When comparing with another platform, match smoothing method and whether the last candle is closed. Do not reinterpret its 15-minute value as a 5-minute or hourly signal.

Separate the time windows

Hypothetical worked example. A 15-minute RSI of 74 can coexist with an hourly RSI of 56. Four 15-minute candles contribute to one hourly candle, but an hourly RSI is not the average of their RSI values. Each calculation uses its own sequence of closes.

Can I infer an hourly confirmation here?

No. The scanner calculates its RSI on 15-minute closes. Use a separate chart with the same method to inspect another timeframe rather than relabeling the scanner value.
